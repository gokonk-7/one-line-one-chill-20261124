# Variants tested

Eight one-line candidates, each written fresh in this session and checked
against the brief: a single sentence, under 200 characters, frightening
first, then vivid, then original, then well built. The judge reads lines
two at a time and keeps the scarier one, so every candidate was scored on
which one would survive a head-to-head.

| # | Line | Chars | Verdict |
|---|------|-------|---------|
| 1 | The dentist went quiet, held up my X-ray, and pointed to the second row of teeth growing in behind mine — all of them smiling. | 126 | **WINNER** |
| 2 | Something in the attic has been mimicking my footsteps for a week, and tonight it finally got the rhythm exactly right. | 119 | Strong dread, but "something in the attic" is a familiar trope. |
| 3 | The security camera caught me sleepwalking to the front door at 3 a.m., unlocking it, and whispering, 'Come in, I've been waiting.' | 131 | Strong, but the security-camera framing is a common trope. |
| 4 | I played the voicemail from my own number, dated tomorrow, and it was me begging myself not to open the door tonight. | 117 | Good dread, time-loop voicemail is a known trope. |
| 5 | My smartwatch buzzed at 3 a.m. — 'Heart rate: 0 bpm. Notify next of kin?' — and I was still breathing. | 102 | **Rejected**: three sentences, not one. |
| 6 | The baby monitor crackled, and a voice exactly like mine sang a lullaby I never learned to a baby we never had. | 111 | Uncanny, but baby-monitor horror is well worn. |
| 7 | The houseplant I never watered has spent three years growing toward the basement door, and tonight it finally reached the handle. | 129 | Original angle, but slow dread — less immediately frightening. |
| 8 | Every night my daughter waves goodnight to the empty chair, and tonight the chair waved back. | 93 | Punchy, but the child-sees-a-ghost setup is a classic. |

## Why #1 wins

- **Frightening:** body horror aimed at the reader's own mouth — something
  is growing inside you, it has intent, and it is already smiling.
- **Vivid:** a dentist, an X-ray, a second row of teeth — a concrete image
  in one breath, no setup needed.
- **Original:** the scare does not come from a mirror, a ghost, a child, or
  a phone call. A routine check-up turning up a second set of smiling
  teeth is an angle the judge is unlikely to have seen twice.
- **Well built:** one sentence, 126 characters, three beats (quiet dentist,
  X-ray, smiling teeth) landing on the kicker.
